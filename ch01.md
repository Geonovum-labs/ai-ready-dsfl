# Introduction

## Introduction
Major challenges in the Netherlands, including housing construction, the energy transition and climate adaptation, compete for scarce space. They can only be tackled together, and that requires reliable, connected information about the physical living environment. The Ministry of the Interior and Kingdom Relations (BZK) addresses this through its policy vision Zicht op Nederland or “data-driven collaboration on the physical living environment” [[BZK-ZoN]]. Within that vision, the Data Space Fysieke Leefomgeving (DSFL) programme is building a federated, decentralised network. In it, government, businesses and other parties share area-based information under common agreements, with location as the connecting key. At the same time, AI is quickly changing how this information is found, combined and interpreted. The EU’s “Unlocking data for AI” strategy [[EU-AIS]] and the AI Act [[EU-AIACT]] treat data spaces as key suppliers of trustworthy data for AI. However, AI applications and agents only work reliably when data carries enough context: clear semantics, quality and uncertainty, provenance, versioning and explicit usage rights. Today, DSFL data products do not meet these conditions in a standardised way, and there is no controlled way for AI agents to access them. The AI ready DSFL project closes this gap. It will explore AI-ready profiles and a Model Context Protocol (MCP) profile for important DSFL data sources, in particular BAG, BGT, BRO and DSO and test how geospatial data can underpin spatial world models. This lays the groundwork for trustworthy spatial intelligence in the physical living environment [[BZK-DSFL]].

## Data Space Physical Living Environment
The Data Space Physical Living Environment (DSFL) is one of the programmes under *Zicht op Nederland*, alongside Datafundament and the Digital Twins programme (NLDT). Its goal is to share area-based information and make it available to everyone, so that all parties work from the same spatial picture of an area, both today and in future scenarios. Instead of building a central platform, DSFL is a federated, decentralised network. Data stays at the source, data owners decide who gets access, and location (x/y/z) is the key that links data across domains. DSFL is the next step for the National Geo-information Infrastructure (NGII). It aligns with the Dutch Digitalisation Strategy (NDS), the Federated Data System (FDS), the Environment and Planning Act, the EU Data Act and the European Data Spaces Support Centre (DSSC) framework.

The programme plan covers the following aspects [[BZK-DSFL]]:

- **Purpose and ambition:** breaking down data silos, supporting faster and better decisions on housing, energy and climate adaptation, supporting the Environment and Planning Act, and shifting from "open data" to "open data unless" (driven by NIS2 and the Dutch Cybersecurity Act). It also lays the foundation for national Digital Twins and AI (world models);
- **Governance and trust framework:** ownership by the policy directorates, with the Geo-information Council (GI Beraad) acting as a "coalition of the willing". Public-private cooperation, stable funding and legal or contractual continuity are central. So are enforceable standards and adopting an existing, proven trust framework;
- **Technical building blocks:** an architecture based on DSSC and the FDS profile. It separates a control plane (identity, catalogues, policies, contracts) from a data plane (APIs, geo-standards). The plan includes an AI-ready profile and a Model Context Protocol (MCP) profile. On top of that come value-added services: georeferencing, Digital Twins as a Service, a public-private marketplace, and a catalogue for data and computational models;
- **Portfolio and chain management:** an agile portfolio process with a shared framework for weighing value against complexity. Prioritisation is done at a programming table. Use cases include national security, circular economy, grid congestion, and housing and mobility;
- **Approach and co-creation:** a phased plan for 2026–2030 and a six-step onboarding process for data providers. The work is incremental, using living labs, proofs of concept and early adopters;
- **Community and knowledge platform:** a "community of communities" supported by the *Zicht op Nederland* platform and a dynamic knowledge platform; 
- **Budget, business case and risks:** cost overview, funding and critical success factors.

The *AI ready DSFL* project is part of the **Data Space Physical Living Environment** (*Data Space Fysieke Leefomgeving*, DSFL) programme of the Dutch **Ministry of the Interior and Kingdom Relations** (*Ministerie van Binnenlandse Zaken en Koninkrijksrelaties*, BZK).

## European and National AI Policies

The AI Ready DSFL project follows European and Dutch AI policy, which share the same aim: use AI to benefit society while protecting public values.

At the European level, the Commission's AI strategy set out a human-centric approach to AI. It aims to increase investment and adoption across sectors, prepare for socio-economic change, and create an ethical and legal framework for trustworthy AI [[EU-AIS]]. The Apply AI Strategy builds on this. It shifts the focus from capability to use: it calls for faster AI adoption in strategic sectors and in the public sector, with attention to European technological sovereignty [[EU-AAIS]]. For AI Ready DSFL, this means the project goes beyond experimenting. It works on putting AI into real use in a responsible and trustworthy way.

At the national level, the Dutch government-wide vision on generative AI calls for responsible use that is grounded in public values and human wellbeing. It also encourages safe experimentation and collaboration across government [[BZK-GENAI]]. The government-wide guidance turns this vision into practice for public organisations. Its central principle is that civil servants stay in control when they use generative AI [[BZK-HGENAI]]. AI Ready DSFL follows these principles. Human oversight, transparency and public values guide every AI application the project develops or adopts.

## Objectives

The *AI ready DSFL* project aims to make the Data Space Physical Living Environment (DSFL) ready for safe, reliable and controlled use by AI applications and AI agents. The specific objectives are [[BZK-DSFL]]:

1. **AI-ready profile for DSFL data products:** standardise the context AI needs to use data reliably: clear semantics (definitions, units, coordinate reference systems), quality and uncertainty, versioning, lineage/provenance and explicit usage rights;
2. **MCP profile for DSFL:** establish the Model Context Protocol (MCP) as the standard way for AI agents to access DSFL data products in the data plane (APIs/geoservices, vocabulary hubs, metadata catalogues). Access is controlled through policy-aware authorisation, logging and traceability;
3. **Reference use cases and proof of value:** demonstrate the added value of the AI-ready and MCP profiles in reference use cases. Pilot implementations with at least two parties, including Kadaster (PDOK) and the Netherlands Geological Survey;
4. **World models and Spatial Intelligence:** use testbeds to explore how geospatial data, information models and domain knowledge (for example from Digital Twins) can underpin spatial world models and LLM integration. The goal is to identify which standards are needed and how to apply them.

These objectives and the resulting profiles will be validated further with stakeholders and pilot partners as the project progresses.
